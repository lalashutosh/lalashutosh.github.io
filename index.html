Project / 03
Qiskit DAG Rewriting Quantum Kernels IBM Manila NumPy

Pulse-Accurate Coherent Noise Simulator

Hardware-faithful NISQ simulation of systematic calibration drift on IBM superconducting hardware.

Post-transpilation injection

Noise is injected after hardware mapping — because a logical H gate doesn't exist on real hardware. Perturbations map to actual physical pulses, not abstract logical operations.

Systematic drift, not stochastic

Drift is assigned per-qubit, not per-gate, capturing coherent error accumulation that stochastic models miss entirely.

Target hardware
IBM Manila — Falcon (FakeManilaV2)
Noise model
θ_noisy = θ_ideal + δ per qubit
Efficiency
DAG-based, O(n) complexity
Benchmark
ZZFeatureMap + SVM kernel

Engineering Approach

01
Build end-to-end

From data ingestion to deployment — full technical stacks, not isolated components.

02
Design for failure

Fail safely, expose uncertainty, remain dependable. Real-world constraints over demo performance.

03
Learn & execute fast

Theory connects to engineering. AI systems, HPC training, quantum simulation — converged quickly.

04
Architect for depth

Modular, composable systems with clear separation between retrieval, reasoning, training, and evaluation layers.

"What is the actual failure mode, and how do I engineer against it?"

Each project starts from this question. In the RAG system: hallucination from thin evidence. In the fine-tuning pipeline: knowledge collapse and uncontrolled generation. In the noise simulator: the systematic, non-random nature of hardware error that stochastic models miss. Technical depth is not the goal — it is the consequence of sophisticated engineering.
